Radial geodesics in a spherically symmetric spacetime

A test of scientific publishing on the site using Markdown as source, versioned images, and LaTeX equations.

Osvaldo L. Santos-Pereira · 24 September 2026 · 4 min read

Physics · General Relativity · Notes

This is a demonstration post for testing a scientific blog area on the site. The proposal is to keep the source text in Markdown inside the repository and render mathematics in the browser with MathJax, without changing the current static architecture.

1. Metric and radial motion

Consider a static and spherically symmetric geometry written as

\[ds^2=-A(r)\,dt^2+B(r)\,dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2,\]

where the angular line element is

\[d\Omega^2=d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2.\]

For purely radial motion, \(d\theta=d\phi=0\). A convenient geodesic Lagrangian is therefore

\[\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\left[-A(r)\dot t^2+B(r)\dot r^2\right].\]

2. Constant of motion

Because the Lagrangian has no explicit dependence on \(t\), this coordinate is cyclic. The corresponding canonical momentum is conserved:

\[p_t=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot t}=-A(r)\dot t=-E.\]

Hence,

\[\dot t=\frac{E}{A(r)}.\]

For a massive particle parametrized by proper time, normalization of the four-velocity gives

\[-1=-A(r)\dot t^2+B(r)\dot r^2,\]

and after substituting the constant of motion we obtain

\[B(r)\dot r^2=\frac{E^2}{A(r)}-1.\]

This expression already reduces the radial dynamics to a first-order equation. The example is intentionally short: the purpose is to verify typography, responsive layout, images, bilingual navigation, and mathematical rendering.

3. Editorial workflow

The article has a Markdown source stored beside its rendered version. A post can therefore begin as a short note, grow in scope, and eventually migrate into a technical note or manuscript without losing its version history.
